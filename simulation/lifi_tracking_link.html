What this models on the real build

  • Transmitter: your Circuit Digest circuit, with the laser on a pan-tilt head (two MG90S servos) and a camera mounted 12 cm above the laser. A Raspberry Pi finds ArUco marker ID 7 and moves the servos to keep it centred.
  • Receiver: the marker is printed 12 cm above the solar panel's centre, matching the camera offset, so centring the marker puts the beam on the panel at any distance.
  • Switching: an ESP32 reads the panel's DC voltage. Below 35% of normal for 50 ms it plays the WiFi stream; above 60% for 0.8 s it goes back to LiFi. The OLED shows the active link.
  • WiFi path: the transmitter ESP32 also samples the MAX4466 output (12-bit, 16 kHz) and streams it over UDP. Both streams go through the same 80 ms playout buffer, so the switch completes before any lost LiFi audio reaches the speaker.

Simplification: the PAM8403 is a class-D amplifier, so the laser really sees fast PWM pulses. The solar panel is too slow to follow them and averages them back into audio, which this model shows directly as brightness = bias + gain × audio.
